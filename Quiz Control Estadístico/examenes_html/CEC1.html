  2. Question

    A process has specification limits LSL = 106 and USL = 130. The estimated process standard deviation is σ=4\sigma=4.

    Calculate the potential capability index CpC_p.


    Solution

    Cp=(USL−LSL)/(6σ)=1C_p=(USL-LSL)/(6\sigma)=1.


  3. Question

    A Shewhart control chart has center line 109, UCL = 119 and LCL = 96. A new sample statistic is 123.

    What is the best conclusion?


    1. No action is needed; the point is within limits.
    2. The process variability has decreased.
    3. The process is out of statistical control.
    4. The process is centered and capable.

    Solution

    The plotted point exceeds the UCL, indicating a special cause and loss of statistical control.


  4. Question

    In an inspection of 100 units, 14 defective units were found. The fraction defective is p=0.14p=0.14.

    Which interpretation is correct?


    1. Approximately 14% of the inspected units were defective.
    2. Exactly 86% of future units will be conforming.
    3. The process capability index equals 0.14.
    4. The process is automatically in statistical control.

    Solution

    The fraction defective is the proportion of defective items in the inspected sample.


  5. Question

    A process has Cp=1.9C_p=1.9 and Cpk=1.5C_{pk}=1.5.

    Which conclusion is most appropriate?


    1. The process is out of statistical control.
    2. The process is perfectly centered between specifications.
    3. The specification limits are wider than the process spread because Cpk>CpC_{pk}>C_p.
    4. The process spread is adequate, but the process is not perfectly centered.

    Solution

    When Cpk<CpC_{pk}<C_p, the process has potential capability but is shifted away from the target or center.
