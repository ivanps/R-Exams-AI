  2. Question

    A process has specification limits LSL = 85 and USL = 105. The estimated process standard deviation is σ=2\sigma=2.

    Calculate the potential capability index CpC_p.


    Solution

    Cp=(USL−LSL)/(6σ)=1.667C_p=(USL-LSL)/(6\sigma)=1.667.


  3. Question

    A Shewhart control chart has center line 106, UCL = 120 and LCL = 92. A new sample statistic is 124.

    What is the best conclusion?


    1. The process variability has decreased.
    2. No action is needed; the point is within limits.
    3. The process is centered and capable.
    4. The process is out of statistical control.

    Solution

    The plotted point exceeds the UCL, indicating a special cause and loss of statistical control.


  4. Question

    In an inspection of 200 units, 27 defective units were found. The fraction defective is p=0.135p=0.135.

    Which interpretation is correct?


    1. The process is automatically in statistical control.
    2. The process capability index equals 0.135.
    3. Approximately 13.5% of the inspected units were defective.
    4. Exactly 86.5% of future units will be conforming.

    Solution

    The fraction defective is the proportion of defective items in the inspected sample.


  5. Question

    A process has Cp=1.8C_p=1.8 and Cpk=1.2C_{pk}=1.2.

    Which conclusion is most appropriate?


    1. The process is out of statistical control.
    2. The specification limits are wider than the process spread because Cpk>CpC_{pk}>C_p.
    3. The process spread is adequate, but the process is not perfectly centered.
    4. The process is perfectly centered between specifications.

    Solution

    When Cpk<CpC_{pk}<C_p, the process has potential capability but is shifted away from the target or center.
